Mobile Oil Change that comes to you

Here's exactly what happens on a standard oil change visit. Containment mats go down first so the driveway stays clean, then the drain plug comes out, the old oil drains fully, and the filter is swapped for a quality replacement matched to your engine.

  • Drain old oil completely and inspect the drain plug and gasket
  • Install a new quality oil filter
  • Refill to factory spec capacity with full synthetic only
  • Reset the oil-life monitor where applicable
  • Quick visual under the vehicle while we're down there, leaks, boots, exhaust hangers
  • Top off washer fluid and check coolant/brake reservoirs
  • Used oil and filter packed out and recycled at a proper facility
  • Log your mileage and oil grade so the next interval is dialed in

Signs you need an oil change

  • Oil-life percentage on the dash is at or near zero
  • Maintenance Required or wrench light is on
  • It's been 5,000 to 7,500 miles since your last synthetic change
  • It's been 3,000 to 5,000 miles on full synthetic oil
  • Oil on the dipstick looks black, gritty, or smells burnt
  • You hear a faint ticking on cold start that fades once oil pressure builds

Why a mobile mechanic makes sense for an oil change

An oil change is the single most common service a car needs, and it's also the one most people put off because of the drive, the wait, and the upsell pitch in the lobby. Mobile flips that. Jack rolls up, you hand off the keys (or don't, you can keep working), and 15 minutes later it's done. No lobby coffee, no "while we have it on the rack" sales pitch, no shuttle ride.

Arizona heat is hard on oil. Summer pavement runs well over 140°F and engine bays get brutal. Full synthetic handles that thermal load far better, which is why most West Valley vehicles run synthetic on a 5,000–7,500 mile interval. Jack will recommend what your manual calls for, no upsell games.
